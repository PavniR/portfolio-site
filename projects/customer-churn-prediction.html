Customer Churn Analytics & Prediction

An end-to-end customer retention intelligence solution that combines SQL analytics, machine learning, explainable AI, interactive dashboards, and deployment to identify high-risk customers before they churn.

Business Problem

Customer churn was identified only after customers had already left.

The bank relied on historical reporting to understand churn, but lacked a proactive way to identify customers at risk before they left. Without early warning signals, retention campaigns were reactive, resources were spread across low-risk customers, and opportunities to prevent churn were frequently missed.

Objectives

What the solution needed to deliver.

  • Identify customers with the highest probability of churn before they leave.
  • Understand the key business drivers influencing customer churn.
  • Prioritize customers using a risk score for targeted retention campaigns.
  • Provide transparent, explainable predictions that business teams can trust.

Dataset Overview

What went into the model.

The project analyzes 10,000 retail banking customers with demographic, financial, behavioral, and account information. Features include credit score, age, tenure, balance, products owned, activity status, geography, complaints, satisfaction score, and customer engagement metrics.

The dataset was transformed into a production-ready feature set through preprocessing, categorical encoding, feature selection, and leakage removal before model development.

Business Analytics

What the data revealed about customer churn.

SQL and exploratory analysis uncovered several business patterns that directly influenced the modelling strategy. Older customers, inactive members, and customers holding multiple banking products exhibited substantially higher churn rates. Complaint records produced unrealistically strong predictive performance, revealing target leakage and leading to their exclusion from the production model.
These insights shaped both the final feature selection and the recommendations presented to business stakeholders.

predictive Modelling

Building a realistic churn prediction system.

Multiple classification models were evaluated, including Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest. Rather than selecting the highest accuracy model, the final solution prioritized realistic business performance by removing data leakage and balancing precision with recall.
SHAP explainability was integrated to explain individual predictions, generate customer-specific risk factors, and improve trust in model decisions.

Key Findings

What mattered most for customer retention.

  • Older customers showed consistently higher churn risk than younger segments.
  • Inactive members were significantly more likely to leave the bank.
  • Customers owning three or more products displayed unexpectedly high churn rates.
  • Germany recorded the highest churn rate among all customer regions.
  • Customer complaints represented target leakage and were excluded from the deployed model.

Business Recommendations

How retention efforts can become proactive.

  • Prioritize high-risk customers using churn probability and risk score rather than treating every customer equally.
  • Launch personalized engagement campaigns for inactive customers before churn occurs.
  • Review customer experience for customers with multiple banking products.
  • Monitor churn trends by geography and age to support targeted retention strategies.
  • Use SHAP explanations to help relationship managers understand why a customer is at risk before contacting them.

Challenges Faced

Building a model that could actually be deployed.

One of the biggest challenges was identifying and removing target leakage caused by the complaint feature, which produced unrealistically high model performance. Another challenge involved balancing precision and recall so the model remained useful for real retention campaigns instead of simply maximizing accuracy. Finally, SHAP explanations were translated into business-friendly language so non-technical users could understand every prediction.

Future Improvements

Extending the solution beyond a portfolio project.

Future iterations could incorporate live banking transaction data, automated retraining pipelines, customer lifetime value for retention prioritization, and continuous model monitoring to detect performance drift. Integrating CRM systems would also enable relationship managers to receive real-time churn alerts and personalized retention recommendations.

Key Takeaways

The short version.

What business problem did this solve?

Enabled proactive customer retention by identifying customers most likely to churn before they leave, allowing targeted interventions instead of reactive campaigns.

What technical skills did I strengthen?

If I rebuilt this today, what would I improve?
