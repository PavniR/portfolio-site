Case Study

Supply Chain Analytics & Inventory Optimization

Built an end-to-end cloud analytics platform that transforms 91k+ supply chain records into actionable inventory insights through SQL analytics, ABC-Pareto analysis, inventory optimization, and automated reporting.

Dataset91K+ Supply Chain Transactions, 50 SKUs, 5 Warehouses, 12 Months
TechnologiesPython, SQL, PostgreSQL/supabase, Streamlit, Plotly
GitHub Repository Live Dashboard

Business Problem

Ops teams lacked a clear view of inventory health and replenishment priorities.

Inventory decisions were based on static reports that made it difficult to identify high-value products, detect overstocking, and prioritize replenishment across warehouses. The objective was to build a centralized analytics platform that transformed operational data into actionable inventory decisions. It allows to simulate inventory scenarios and instantly calculate safety stock, reorder points, recommended order quantities, and inventory status.

Objectives

What the platform needed to deliver.

  • Centralize operational reporting into a single analytics platform.
  • Prioritize high-value inventory using ABC-Pareto analysis.
  • Generate replenishment recommendations using inventory optimization.
  • Automate reporting through a cloud-hosted PostgreSQL backend and ETL pipeline.
  • Provide interactive dashboards for operational decision-making.

Dataset Overview

What went into the model.

The project began with 91,000+ supply chain transactions containing SKU, warehouse, sales, inventory, lead time, demand, and profitability information. Additional analytical datasets were created through feature engineering to support inventory optimization and executive reporting.

Rather than querying raw transactional data directly, the workflow produced curated analytical tables for KPI reporting, inventory recommendations, and forecasting experiments before storing them in a cloud PostgreSQL database.

Data Cleaning

Preparing data for reliable analysis.

Validated raw operational records, engineered inventory metrics, standardized data types, and transformed transactional data into analytics-ready datasets that powered executive reporting and inventory optimization.

Understanding operational performance.

Where the delays actually were.

Initial analysis focused on warehouse performance, product profitability, inventory distribution, and revenue contribution. These findings identified where operational attention should be concentrated before optimization models were introduced.

Business Insights

What the data actually said.

TThe analysis suggests that improving inventory performance is less about holding more stock and more about prioritizing the right stock.

  • ABC-Pareto analysis showed that a small subset of SKUs generated most of the business value, making them the highest priority for inventory planning and service-level protection.
  • Overstocking represented the most common inventory issue, tying up working capital without improving product availability.
  • Inventory health varied considerably across warehouses, highlighting opportunities to rebalance stock instead of increasing overall inventory.
  • Centralizing operational KPIs made inventory risks significantly easier to identify.

Business Recommendations

What ops should do differently.

  • Prioritize Category A products with stricter inventory monitoring and higher service-level targets to minimize business impact from stockouts.
  • Shift replenishment planning from fixed reorder rules to data-driven reorder points calculated from demand variability and lead times.
  • Review persistently overstocked SKUs for procurement adjustments, warehouse transfers, or promotional strategies to reduce carrying costs.

Challenge Faced:

One experimental component of the project involved demand forecasting using machine learning. Although the models produced reasonable predictions, forecast accuracy was not consistently reliable enough for operational inventory planning. Rather than deploying a model with limited business value, the final solution focused on deterministic inventory optimization using safety stock and reorder point calculations. The forecasting workflow remains in the repository for future iteration.

Future Improvements

How this would evolve in production.

Future iterations would integrate live ERP/WMS data through scheduled ingestion pipelines and revisit demand forecasting using longer historical windows, richer external features, and probabilistic forecasting methods.

Inventory recommendations could then combine forecasted demand with current stock positions for more dynamic replenishment planning.
